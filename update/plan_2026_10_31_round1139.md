## Findings

- #42781 lets a remote exec server connect directly to AWS-hosted registries:
  `--remote-transport direct` registers the `direct_jsonrpc_v1` transport,
  signs registration and WebSocket handshakes with SigV4, reuses the
  registration across transient disconnects, refreshes it after a `409
  Conflict`, and requires TLS for non-loopback endpoints. Noise stays the
  default transport.

## Implementation

- `execserver/remote_direct.go`: `DirectTransport`, `RemoteEnvironmentTransport`,
  `DirectHandshakeSigner`, `registerDirectEnvironment`,
  `runDirectEnvironment`, `dialDirectEnvironment`,
  `authenticateDirectWebSocketRequest`, and the retry/TLS helpers. The
  handshake is signed through an `https`/`http` projection of the WebSocket
  URL, the authenticated URL (including presigned query parameters) is used
  for the dial, and handshake headers cannot be rewritten by the signer.
- `execserver/remote.go`: `RemoteEnvironmentConfig.Transport` and
  `DirectSigner`; `RunRemoteEnvironment` dispatches to the direct loop and
  `WithExecutorCredentialAuth` rejects the direct transport like Rust.
- `cli/cli.go`: `--remote-transport`, `--aws-sigv4`, `--aws-profile`,
  `--aws-region`, and `--aws-service` (default `execute-api`) with Rust's
  requires/conflicts validation, and `app/app.go` builds the SigV4 signer from
  `auth.LoadAWSAuthContext` and never consults the account store in direct mode.
- Tests: `execserver/remote_direct_like_rust_test.go` (registration validation,
  host-only signing, authenticated URL query parameters, JSON-RPC `initialize`
  over the direct WebSocket, registration reuse, 409 re-registration, retry
  classification, TLS/loopback) and
  `cli/exec_server_remote_transport_test.go` (defaults, parsing,
  requires/conflicts).

## Assessment

- The Go CLI rejects `exec-server forward` combined with `--remote` outright, so
  Rust's extra "direct transport does not support forwarding" check is
  unreachable but retained; the earlier rejection is stricter.
