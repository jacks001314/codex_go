## Goal

Audit #42378 (route rollout reads through the canonical JSON decoder) and verify
that decimal token-count payloads do not block paginated resume.

## Findings

- Rust's direct `RolloutLine` deserialization used a flattened enum and could
  reject decimal numbers nested in a token-count record. The fix decodes the
  line into `serde_json::Value` first and routes every reader through that
  canonical decoder helper.
- Go's persisted `Line` envelope is not flattened and keeps `payload`, `item`,
  `metadata`, and other item bodies as `json.RawMessage`. Token-count usage and
  rate-limit values therefore remain untyped until a consumer decodes them,
  and existing consumers use maps for those decimal fields.
- `rollout.Load`, `Resume`, discovery, history materialization, and previews all
  share that raw `Line` representation; there is no alternate typed rollout
  deserializer to route through a new helper.
- Existing tests already preserve fractional values in loaded payloads and in
  token-count metadata replay. Paginated ordinal continuation after a decimal
  token-count line was the remaining untested contract.

## Implementation

- `rollout/projection_test.go`: added a paginated-resume regression that appends
  a token-count record with `12.5` and `0.000000000001` rate-limit percentages,
  resumes the recorder, and verifies the next ordinal is 3 while both decimal
  values survive in the raw payload.

## Decision

- Mark `69cebb5d15` as `complete`: the structural flattening failure cannot
  occur with Go's raw envelope, and the resume/ordinal contract is now covered.
