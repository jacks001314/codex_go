## Findings

- #43031 moves Apps tool-catalog state onto the client that fetched it. A
  refreshed Apps catalog keeps its tools and revision while the connection is
  reused, cached bindings are keyed by client identity plus revision, and a
  refresh publishes only after calls prepared against the previous revision
  finish. Prepared calls for unrelated servers stay valid.
- `CodexThread::refresh_codex_apps_tools` reconciles the runtime and returns the
  exact client's raw inventory plus the tool names eligible under the runtime's
  generic MCP policy. `app/installed` uses that snapshot; the post-elicitation
  connector-auth path keeps using the whole-runtime `hard_refresh`.

## Implementation

- `mcp/client_tool_catalog.go`: `clientToolCatalog` with an `RWMutex`-backed
  snapshot, a serialized `refresh` that advances the revision only on success,
  and `runWithRevision`, which holds catalog authority for the whole call.
- `mcp/http_client.go` / `mcp/stdio_client.go`: each live connection lazily owns
  one catalog, so reconnects start at revision zero and pooled stdio helpers
  share the catalog of their exact connection.
- `mcp/codex_apps_catalog.go`: `CodexAppsToolSnapshot`,
  `RefreshCodexAppsTools`, `refreshClientToolCatalog` (reuses the exact client
  and lists tools only), `liveClientToolCatalog`, and
  `publishClientCatalogTools`.
- `mcp/binding.go`: bindings capture each server's client catalog and revision,
  prefer the client's inventory once it has been refreshed, and execute calls
  under the captured revision. Unrelated servers keep revision zero and are not
  invalidated by an Apps refresh.
- `appserver/apps_mcp_accessible.go`: a forced app refresh publishes only the
  Apps client's catalog; a failed refresh falls back to the retained inventory
  instead of clearing clients or bumping the service generation.
