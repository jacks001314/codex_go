## Goal

Audit #42320 (make the app-server thread unload delay configurable) and close
any remaining behavior gap.

## Findings

- Go already recognizes the top-level `thread_unload_delay_secs` key and
  `Config.ThreadUnloadDelaySecs()` defaults to 60 seconds.
- The runtime idle lifecycle already supports zero-delay unloading, per-thread
  start/fork overrides, subscriber and activity reset, active-turn preservation,
  `thread/closed` plus `thread/status/changed` notifications, and cold-resume
  reattachment (`appserver/thread_idle_lifecycle.go`,
  `appserver/runtime_router.go`).
- The remaining divergence was load-time validation: Rust rejects a value that
  cannot fit a monotonic-clock deadline, while the Go accessor silently fell
  back to 60 seconds. Negative, fractional, and non-numeric values were likewise
  accepted by the effective loader and then ignored by the accessor.

## Implementation

- `config/config.go`: effective config loading now validates
  `thread_unload_delay_secs` as an optional non-negative integer and rejects
  deadlines above `math.MaxInt64/time.Second` with
  ``thread_unload_delay_secs is too large``.
- `appserver/thread_idle_lifecycle_test.go`: added a regression test that a
  subscriber arriving after a zero-delay unload was scheduled cancels the
  countdown, and unsubscribing later re-arms it.
- `config/thread_unload_delay_like_rust_test.go`: covers negative, fractional,
  string, and overflow values at effective load, plus a valid zero override.
