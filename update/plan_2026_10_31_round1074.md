## Goal

Keep a Guardian review's evidence internally consistent and reject an allow
decision whose authorization changed while the review ran.

## Findings

- Rust captures one parent history snapshot for checkpoint selection and prompt
  evidence when `GuardianThreadContext` is enabled, and cancels a completed allow
  when the owning session's user-message revision or the root authorization
  version changed during the review, even without cacheable review evidence.
- Go's reviewer keeps a single per-attempt snapshot: the prompt and the
  post-run comparison both come from `guardianReviewTranscriptSelection` /
  `guardianTranscriptWithoutProposedCall`, and
  `guardianReviewContextChanged` re-checks the transcript entries, omission
  notice and root user authorization after the model responds.
- When that predicate reports a change, Go only invalidates a parsed allow
  (`state.OutcomeAllow`); a deny stands on the evidence it saw. Changed
  authorization triggers a fresh attempt without service backoff, and an
  exhausted retry budget ends in `failed_closed` with reason
  `stale_authorization` instead of applying a stale approval.
- `guardian_retry_like_rust_test.go` covers the stale retry, the deny
  short-circuit, and the no-backoff refresh; `guardian_user_message_revision_like_rust_test.go`
  covers revision-based invalidation of serving authorization.

## Implementation

- None required. The upstream invariants already exist in the Go reviewer's
  context-change predicate and failure-closed path.
